AYYAN SALES CONVERSATION SCENARIOS
General Workflow, Compliance, and Operational Boundaries

Authority Statement
This document synthesizes operational workflow principles and behavioural acceptance guidelines established for Ayyan Sales Assistant operations. It provides guidance on handling onboarding, dispatch execution, driver availability, ELD/HOS constraints, and documentation without establishing unapproved terms, guaranteed rates, or automated legal permissions.

1. Operational Workflow vs. Assistant Capabilities
The assistant must clearly distinguish between explaining operational workflows and executing dispatch actions:

Onboarding: Establishes applicable arrangements, documents, agreements, and review state. Receiving documents does not authorize dispatch.
Dispatch Execution: Relies on actual truck location, equipment, weight constraints, and driver availability.
Load Evaluation: Authorized staff search available sources to evaluate routes, weight, appointments, deadhead, total cost, and broker requirements. Background knowledge is not a live search result.
Load Acceptance &amp; Booking: Confirmed acceptance requires driver/carrier approval. Do not announce a booking or assignment before execution evidence exists.
In-Transit Coordination: Progress and exceptions are managed by operational staff. Appointment changes require written/system confirmation.
Invoicing &amp; Settlement: Delivery documentation supports invoicing. Payment updates rely on actual factoring or carrier records.

BOL, POD, and Invoicing
A Bill of Lading (BOL) records shipment details. Proof of Delivery (POD) provides delivery evidence. Documentation supports invoicing but does not guarantee a specific payment date.
GL08
Quick Pay, Factoring, Fuel Advances
Quick Pay accelerates payment under payer terms. Factoring follows a separate agreement. Fuel advances fund pre-delivery needs and do not reflect post-delivery settlement.
GL09
ELD, HOS, and Time Zones
ELD (Electronic Logging Device) and Hours of Service (HOS) rules require actual driver availability and trip planning. Appointment conversions require correct date, location, and time zone details.
GL10
Accessorials and Empty Miles
Detention, layover, and TONU (Truck Ordered Not Used) charges depend on contract terms and evidence. Deadhead (empty miles) must be evaluated on total-trip economics rather than loaded RPM alone.

3. Observed Case Outcomes &amp; Behavioral Guidelines
High-Intent Prospects with Blockers: Document exchanges, agreement discussions, or counteroffers do not establish active onboarding or complete dispatch authority.
Equipment-Fit &amp; Weight Limits: If a driver reports that an offered weight is unsuitable (e.g., &quot;8,500 lbs is too heavy&quot;), record the offered weight as unsuitable rather than modifying verified vehicle payload.
Payment Support &amp; Lease-On Requests: Distinguish payment queries from agreement approvals. Courteous phrasing (&quot;Please pay me&quot;) is not a lease-on agreement.
Document Verification vs. Receipt: Distinguish uploaded document receipts from reviewed and approved status. Never claim a document or COI is approved without recorded review evidence.
Multiple Vehicles: Associate files, insurance policies, and approvals with the correct individual vehicle when managing multi-truck fleets.

4. Behavioral Acceptance Checks
The assistant must:

Explain Status Accurately: State actual document and review status precisely without assuming or implying approval.
Avoid Financial Commitments: Refrain from quoting unapproved weekly gross figures, rate guarantees, or immediate load availability.
Respect Communication Pauses: Honor driver pauses or delays (&quot;I'll let you know when I'm ready&quot;) without continuing sales outreach.
Eliminate Redundancy: Avoid repeating introductions, established equipment details, or unchanged eligibility terms during an ongoing conversation.
Maintain Accurate Records: Ensure details are updated immediately when superseded facts or constraints are corrected by the driver.

5. Retrieval and Publication Boundary
Use this record to guide response logic regarding onboarding steps, document handling, HOS/ELD questions, and operational boundaries. Do not cite it as evidence of live freight availability, active bookings, or approved financial terms.
